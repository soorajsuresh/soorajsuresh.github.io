If $L$ is a line and $A$ and $B$ are distinct points on $L$, then $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is called a \defn{vector along $L$}.

A line $L$ is said to be \defn{parallel} to a nonzero vector $\d$ if a vector along $L$ is parallel to $\d$. 

If $L$ is parallel to $\d$, then $\d$ is called a \defn{direction vector} of $L$ and $\frac{\d}{\abs{\d}}$ is called the \defn{direction of $L$}.

A line $L$ in space passing through the point $P_0=(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ with a direction vector $\d=a\i+b\j+c\k$ is the set \[L=\build{(x,y,z)}{x=x_0+ta,\quad y=y_0+tb,\quad z=z_0+tc,\quad t\in(-\infty,\infty)}.\]

The equations \[x=x_0+ta,\quad y=y_0+tb,\quad z=z_0+tc\] are called \defn{parametric equations of $L$}.

The equation \[x\i+y\j+z\k=\parens{x_0+ta}\i+\parens{y_0+tb}\j+\parens{z_0+tc}\k\] is called a \defn{vector equation of $L$}.

Letting $\r$ denote $x\i+y\j+z\k$ and $\r_0$ denote $x_0\i+y_0\j+z_0\k$, the vector equation of $L$ is \[\r=\r_0+t\d.\]
