Lines in Space

If $L$ is a line and $A$ and $B$ are distinct points on $L$, then $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is called a vector along $L$.

A line $L$ is said to be parallel to a nonzero vector $\d$ if a vector along $L$ is parallel to $\d$.

If $L$ is parallel to $\d$, then $\d$ is called a direction vector of $L$ and $\frac{\d}{\abs{\d}}$ is called the direction of $L$.

A line $L$ in space passing through the point $P_0=(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ with a direction vector $\d=a\i+b\j+c\k$ is the set

$L=\build{(x\text{, }y\text{, }z)}{x=x_0+ta\text{, }\quad y=y_0+tb\text{, }\quad z=z_0+tc\text{, }\quad t\in(-\infty\text{, }\infty)}\text{. }$

The equations

$x=x_0+ta\text{, }\quad y=y_0+tb\text{, }\quad z=z_0+tc$

The equation

$x\i+y\j+z\k=\parens{x_0+ta}\i+\parens{y_0+tb}\j+\parens{z_0+tc}\k$

Letting $\r$ denote $x\i+y\j+z\k$ and $\r_0$ denote $x_0\i+y_0\j+z_0\k$, the vector equation of $L$ is

$\r=\r_0+t\d\text{. }$
